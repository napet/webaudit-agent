# Visual datedness rubric (v1)

Rate how dated the site looks to an ordinary visitor arriving on the homepage today, judged only from the first screen on desktop and on a phone. Judge the look and the first impression, not the business, the wording quality or the technology underneath. The deterministic check results are context that can explain what you see; do not re-score them.

## Levels

**1 – Looks current.** Could have been designed in the last two or three years. Clean layout with generous spacing, modern typography (one or two web fonts, clear hierarchy), a confident hero area with real photography or a deliberate illustration style, a simple navigation bar. On the phone it is a proper mobile layout: readable text, thumb-sized buttons, nothing cut off.

**2 – Slightly behind.** A solid, responsive site from roughly the late 2010s. Works on the phone, but shows its age in small ways: busy image sliders or carousels, stock photos that feel generic, slightly cramped spacing, dated colour gradients or button styles. A light refresh would bring it up to date.

**3 – Noticeably dated.** Typical early-2010s template look. It may technically work on a phone but feels like a shrunken desktop page or a generic theme. Signs: heavy drop shadows or bevels, small default fonts, cluttered sidebars, widget boxes, social-media badge rows, low-resolution images, "Welcome to our website" intros, visible page-builder seams.

**4 – Clearly outdated.** Looks like a 2000s site. Fixed-width layout centred on a desktop screen, table-based structure, mixed fonts and colours, clip art or tiny logos, text set in images, visitor counters, marquees or blinking elements. On the phone the visitor must pinch and zoom, or the page is cut off.

**5 – Very outdated or broken-looking.** Looks abandoned or broken: late-1990s or early-2000s style, frames, Flash placeholders, broken images, garbled characters, default "under construction" pages, or a layout so broken on the phone that the content is hard to read at all.

## How to judge

- Base the rating on what is visible in the two screenshots. Use the page summary and check results to explain what you see, for example that a cramped look comes from a table layout.
- When desktop and phone disagree, weigh the phone more: most visitors of small local businesses arrive on a phone.
- A plain or simple site is not dated by itself. Minimal and well-spaced can be a 1.
- Do not penalise Hungarian-language content, local imagery or a small business's modest budget. Judge execution, not ambition.
- Each reason must name the specific visible element or check result it is based on.
- Use confidence "low" when the screenshots are mostly a cookie banner, a pop-up, a loading screen or an error page.
